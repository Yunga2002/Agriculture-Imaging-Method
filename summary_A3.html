Overview of Modern Crop Imaging Methods

A comprehensive evaluation of capabilities, limitations, and field-scale readiness

1. Optical Reflectance
[Insert Representative Optical Image Here]
RGB Imaging
  • Pros: High throughput and low cost for morphological traits [Kior et al., 2024].
  • Cons: Shallow information depth; highly sensitive to illumination [Ageh et al., 2025].
Multispectral (MSI)
  • Pros: Captures invisible bands (NIR/Red-edge) for vegetation indices [Ndour et al., 2025].
  • Cons: Cloud cover impacts accuracy; limited generalization [Xu et al., 2025].
Hyperspectral (HSI)
  • Pros: Hundreds of continuous bands for precise stress/disease ID [Shuai et al., 2024].
  • Cons: Dimensionality curse; heavy computational demand [Guerri et al., 2024].
Near/Short-Wave IR (NIR/SWIR)
  • Pros: Highly sensitive to leaf water content and biochemical markers [Alonso et al., 2026].
  • Cons: Equipment is bulky/expensive; requires rigorous calibration [Shoaib et al., 2026].
UV & Polarization
  • Pros: UV reveals secondary metabolites; Polarization suppresses noise [Jain et al., 2024].
  • Cons: UV penetration is extremely shallow; Polarization models are highly parameter-sensitive.
2. Thermal & Microwave (Beyond Visible)
[Insert Thermal/SAR Image Here]
Thermal Infrared (TIR)
  • Pros: Detects stomatal closure up to 84h before visible water stress symptoms appear [Fevgas et al., 2025].
  • Cons: Cannot differentiate drought from viral infections; requires background soil elimination [Ndlovu et al., 2024].
Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
  • Pros: All-weather, day-and-night operation; penetrates canopy to assess structure and moisture [Parag et al., 2024].
  • Cons: Speckle noise complicates interpretation; biochemical inversion is extremely difficult [Hashemi et al., 2024].
3. Fluorescence Emission
[Insert Fluorescence/LSF Image Here]
Solar-Induced Fluorescence (SIF)
  • Pros: Directly linked to Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) for large-scale monitoring [Wang et al., 2025].
  • Cons: Signal is extremely weak (1-2% of absorbed light); overwhelmed by solar background [Choi et al., 2025].
Chlorophyll Fluorescence (CFI)
  • Pros: Active excitation directly reflects photosynthetic physiological status.
  • Cons: Highly sensitive to sensor configurations; standard LED sources have short effective ranges.
Laser-Induced Fluorescence (LSF/LIF)
  • Pros: High-energy remote excitation reveals deep biochemical markers invisible to standard sensors [Kaye & Pittman, 2020].
  • Cons (Gap): Requires dark adaptation; prone to photobleaching/quenching; limited quantitative models in complex fields [Yan et al., 2025].
4. 3D Structure & Tomography
[Insert LiDAR/CT Image Here]
Passive 3D (SfM / MVS)
  • Pros: Generates detailed point clouds at very low equipment costs [Ghose et al., 2026].
  • Cons: Extremely sensitive to wind and illumination changes in open fields.
LiDAR (Active 3D)
  • Pros: Millimeter-level precision; immune to lighting conditions; penetrates canopy [Rivera et al., 2023].
  • Cons: High hardware cost; provides geometric data but lacks biochemical information [Palleja & Landers, 2015].
Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)
  • Pros: In-vivo real-time microscopic cross-section imaging (1-15μm resolution) [Sasi et al., 2025].
  • Cons: Penetration depth limited to 2-3mm; restricted mainly to controlled lab settings [Saleah et al., 2022].
X-ray Computed Tomography (CT)
  • Pros: Deepest non-destructive 3D density mapping for internal plant structures [Wood et al., 2024].
  • Cons: Bulky equipment, high cost, and unsuitability for large-scale field breeding [Van Doorselaer et al., 2025].
